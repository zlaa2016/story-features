“Grandma,” the young girl said, her brow  furrowed in thought. “I was talking to my   friend at school today, and she said it doesn’t  matter if we try to help people because there   are just too many problems in the world. Is  that true? Does it really matter if we try?” Grandma paused for a moment, her eyes twinkling.  “That’s a big question, my dear. It reminds   me of a story I once heard—a story about a  little boy, the ocean, and some starfish.” The girl looked curious. “A boy  and starfish? What happened?” “Let me tell you,” Grandma said, settling  in comfortably. “It’s a lovely tale…” Once upon a time, on a beautiful, sunny  morning, a little boy walked along the shore   of a beach. The tide had gone out, leaving  hundreds of starfish stranded on the sand,   scattered across the beach as far as he could  see. The boy knew that without the water,   the starfish would dry out in  the sun and eventually die. Feeling a sense of urgency, he bent down,  carefully picked up a starfish, and tossed it back   into the ocean. He watched as it splashed back  into the water, feeling proud of his small act. But then he looked around and saw the  countless starfish lying helplessly on   the sand. He felt a pang of sadness in his heart. As he continued to throw starfish back into  the ocean, a man, who had been watching him   from a distance, shook his head and walked  over. “What are you doing, young one?” he   asked. “There are thousands of starfish on this  beach. You can’t possibly make a difference.” The boy looked up at the man for a moment, his  eyes wide with determination. Then he bent down,   picked up another starfish, and threw it  into the water. “I made a difference for   that one,” he said simply, his voice unwavering. The man stood there, taken aback by the boy’s  response. He was about to argue when he noticed   how focused the boy was, how each starfish was  important to him. After a moment of silence,   he too bent down, picked up a starfish,  and tossed it back into the sea. As the boy continued his work, others who were  walking along the beach began to notice. They   saw the boy throwing starfish back into the ocean,  one by one, and something sparked in their hearts.   Soon, more people joined in, bending down to  pick up starfish and returning them to the water. Before long, dozens of people were on the  beach, each one helping to save the starfish,   one at a time. What had seemed like an impossible  task suddenly became one of hope and unity. The   little boy’s small action inspired others,  and together they made a difference. As Grandma finished the story, the young girl  sat quietly, absorbing the lesson. “So even   though the boy couldn’t save all the starfish,  it still mattered because he helped a few?” Grandma nodded, a smile on her face. “Exactly,  my dear. Sometimes we think we can’t make a   difference because the problems seem too big or  too overwhelming. But helping even one person,   or doing one small thing, can matter in ways we  can’t always see. Just like the boy on the beach.” The girl tilted her head, a thoughtful  expression crossing her face. “So,   it’s not about saving everyone. It’s  about doing what we can, right?” “Right,” Grandma said, her voice  filled with warmth. “You might   feel like your actions are small,  but they can inspire others. Just   think about how that boy’s determination  changed the day for so many starfish.” The girl smiled, her eyes sparkling with newfound  understanding. “I like that, Grandma. Maybe I   can help in small ways too. Like picking up  litter or helping a friend when they’re sad.” “Absolutely!” Grandma replied, proud  of her granddaughter’s insight. “Every   little act of kindness counts. And you never know   how your actions might ripple out  and inspire others to do the same.” The girl looked out the window,   watching the sun peek through the clouds. “I  guess it’s like saving starfish—one at a time.” Grandma reached over and squeezed her  hand gently. “Yes, my dear. Remember,   the world may seem like a big, complicated place,   but you have the power to make a difference,  no matter how small your actions may seem.” As the waves continued to  crash softly against the shore,   the girl leaned against her grandmother,  feeling a sense of comfort and inspiration.
