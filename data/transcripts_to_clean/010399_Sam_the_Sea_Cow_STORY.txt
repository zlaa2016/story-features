Spring comes to Southern Florida. A huge, gray animal moves slowly down a river toward the sea. It is a shy, harmless manatee. On her back, the manatee carries a brand-new calf just born. His name will be Sam. The manatee rises so her calf's head is above the water. (manatee inhaling) The calf breathes in. (manatee exhaling) He breathes out. Even though a manatee lives in the water, it breathes air, just like you and me. Sam's mother ducks him and lifts him and ducks him and lifts him. She teaches him to breathe in the air and hold his breath underwater. Sam finds a place beneath his mother's flipper when he is hungry. He sucks her warm milk. Manatees are mammals, so they feed their babies milk from their bodies. Sam looks like his mother. He has little round eyes and a small head. He hears sound, but he has no outside ears. Sam's body is shaped like a big pear. He swims with his two mitten-shaped flippers. Later, he will learn to use his tail. (manatee calling) One day Sam hears a strange noise. He lifts his head to look 'Eep, eep,' his mother squeals to warn him. Bam, bam, bam. Too late, a motorboat hits Sam. The propeller cuts him. Motorboats are manatees' worst enemies. Sam's wound leaves a scar between his eyes. It will be there for the rest of his life. (insects chirping) In the fall, Sam and his mother swim up a river to warmer waters. Alligators snooze on the shore. Long-legged herons wade in the mud. Sam eats plants, like other manatees. Mm, num, num, num, nom, (manatee licking). Sam pokes his whiskered face into some hyacinth. Munch, munch, munch, munch, munch. (manatee munching) (teeth chomping) Every five minutes, Sam comes up to breathe, and sometimes he pops up near fishing boats and surprises the fishermen. Sam leaves his mother when he is two years old. Like other manatees, he spends the next few years swimming along the sea coast in the warm months and returning to the rivers in the cooler season. When Sam isn't eating, he goes exploring. He swims around wooden pilings. He searches under old piers. One day, Sam swims into a big round cement pipe. He squirms half in, but then he can't get out again. Sam is stuck in a sewer drain. People hear Sam's cries, 'Eep, eep, eep.' They try to help him, but Sam can't be moved from the drain. Someone calls the seaquarium. They pull Sam out of the sewer and take him to the seaquarium. Sam has a new home now. He lives in a round cement pool. Soon Sam is feeling well again, but there is nothing for him to explore here. Even when Sam is put in a pool with other manatees, he is lonely. The keeper's finally decide to return him to a river. Sam is lifted to a truck and taken to an airplane. The pilot flies Sam to Crystal River 600 miles away. Other manatees are swimming in the river. "Eep, eep, eep," Sam calls them. He flips his tail and swims to them. One manatee turns to face Sam. She puts her snout right up to Sam's. They rub noses. They rub whispers. They swim off together. Goodbye, Sam.
