[narrator] Every day Larnel and his mother stepped in to see Mrs. Katz. - Since my husband died, I'm so alone. "That's Myron," said Mrs. Katz, as they looked at the photo album. - We had such a life. Such a life. - [Narrator] Her voice broke. - We had no children, and I'll be alone for Hanukkah and Passover. - [Narrator] The next day, Larnel stepped in to see her himself. - I've been thinking. A cat had some kittens in a basement of our building. Nobody wants this one because she's so ugly.  - [Mrs. Katz] Oh, ho! Scrawny little bobella. Oh, I... I don't know. (piano keys strum) - [Narrator] Then Mrs. Katz saw Larnell's face. - I'll take her, but only if you'll help me with her. (piano keys strum) - [Narrator] Larnel promised.  - "A good Yiddish name I'll give her." said Mrs. Katz. - [Mrs. Katz] She has no tail. And all you see is her tush! Oh, we'll call her Tush!  - [Narrator] Little Tush grew healthy and strong. Mrs. Katz cooked for her, and knitted toys for her. "Such a person!" she'd say as she watched Tush play. Mrs. Katz was in love. (cat meows) - Larnel visited every day, and there was always a fresh baked Kugel, and a tall glass of milk waiting for him. Mrs. Katz talked about the way times used to be. - I came here from Poland. I didn't speak one word of English. Myron and I used to vacation in the Catskills, a Bosch resort, a place for Jews to stay. - You mean Jews couldn't stay anywhere they wanted? My grandma couldn't stay places either. - Larnel, your people and mine are alike. Troubles we've seen, happiness too. And great strengths we've had. - [Narrator] One afternoon, Mrs. Katz said, - Since you are almost family to me, Larnel, I want you to come with me to say Kaddish for my Myron. I know you're not Jewish, but Myron would've liked you. You are such a person, Larnel. - [Narrator] At the cemetery, she read from her book. Then she asked Larnel to put a small rock on Mr. Katz's headstone. - [Mrs. Katz] We do this to remember. (birds chirping) (street noise) - [Narrator] When they got home, Tush wasn't there. - I... I forgot to shut the window! Poor bubalish. She, she's never been outside before! - We'll find her. (piano music) - [Narrator] Mrs. Katz and Larnel left notes on doors, telephone poles and fences. They asked everyone who lived nearby, but no one has seen little Tush. (knocking) The next morning, a loud knock woke up Mrs. Katz. (door opening) It was Larnel's father and two neighbors. - "Is this yours?" They asked. - [Narrator] Mrs. Katz took Tush in her arms. - [Mrs. Katz] My, my bubala! My little catilla! (cat meowing) - [Narrator] One day, Mrs. Katz seemed very lonely and sad and she said, - I miss Myron. Passover this year will be just me. - Could I have Passover dinner with you? - "Oh, I thought you'd never ask!" She exclaimed. Such a Seder I'll prepare for you. - [Narrator] And when they sat down to the Seder together, Mrs. Katz lit candles, read from her book, and said prayers. They drank red wine and water. They ate bitter herbs, Gefilte fish and spicy chopped apples with potato pancakes and Mazza. The next day, Mrs. Katz yelled out her window to Larnel, - Come! Come quickly, already! Something wonderful happened! - [Narrator] Larnel and his mom and dad rushed to the door. - [Mrs. Katz] Mazel tov, Tush! Four babies! Now you are a mommy, and and I am a boba. A grandma! (cat meows) (sentimental piano music) - [Narrator] As the years passed, Mrs. Katz, Tush, and her descendants became part of Larnel's family. There were graduations, weddings, new babies, and, and finally a Kaddish. Larnel stood in front of her headstone. He read from her book. He placed a small rock on top of her headstone. Then Larnel, his wife and their children, read the inscription together. Mrs. Katz, our Bubee... Such a person.
