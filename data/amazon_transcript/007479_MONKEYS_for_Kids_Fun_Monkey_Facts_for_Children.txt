Kind: captions Language: en Who spends their days climbing in trees, leaping&nbsp; high up into the sky or switching without ease? Who calls out in yelps, grunts or screeches? Who lives together in groups we call Troops? Well its monkeys of course! Monkeys live in many different kinds of habitats.&nbsp;&nbsp; They can live on mountains, jungles,&nbsp; grasslands, rain forests, and even in cities! Many monkeys are arboreal monkeys.&nbsp;&nbsp; That means that they live in the trees.&nbsp; Other monkeys live mostly on the ground. Snow monkeys are called macaques. Many&nbsp; of these live in the mountains of Japan.&nbsp;&nbsp; To keep warm in the winter, they bathe in&nbsp; the hot mountain water. The water is heated&nbsp;&nbsp; inside the Earth and bubbles to the surface. Snow&nbsp; monkeys like to relax and play in the warm water. New World monkeys live in parts&nbsp; of Central and South America.&nbsp;&nbsp; They are small, have flat&nbsp; noses, and live in trees.&nbsp; This is a Capuchin monkey. You can find&nbsp; him in parts of Central and South America. Old World monkey live in parts of Africa and Asia.&nbsp; They are larger, and their noses are not flat.&nbsp;&nbsp; They live in mountains, grasslands, towns, and&nbsp; forests. They usually sit on their bottom pads.&nbsp;&nbsp; This is a Vervet monkey. You&nbsp; can find him in parts of Africa. Monkey mothers have a very strong bond with their&nbsp; babies. This bond helps babies learn from them.&nbsp;&nbsp; Monkey babies stay close to&nbsp; their moms for their first year. Young monkeys play together. They&nbsp; love to wrestle and tumble each other.&nbsp;&nbsp; When boys are older, they usually go off&nbsp; to join another troop. Most girl monkeys&nbsp;&nbsp; stay with their moms and then one day, they&nbsp; will eventually raise their own families. Monkeys have strong bonds with the other members&nbsp; of their troop. Grooming is the main way monkeys&nbsp;&nbsp; show they care. Mothers groom babies. Females&nbsp; groom other females, and couples groom each other. Here are some fascinating facts about monkeys— The loudest is called the Howler monkey.&nbsp; We can hear them up to 3 miles away! The tiniest is the pygmy marmoset&nbsp; and is about the size of a banana. The biggest monkey is a male mandrill and&nbsp; can weigh anywhere from 77 to 100 pounds. The furriest monkey is the&nbsp; golden snub-nosed monkey.&nbsp;&nbsp; The fur keeps them warm in the cold&nbsp; mountains of China where they live.
