Once upon a time, a merchant asked his three&nbsp; daughters what he should bring them from the&nbsp;&nbsp; city. The first asked for pearls, the&nbsp; second for gold, but the youngest longed&nbsp;&nbsp; for a singing lark. The merchant found&nbsp; a pearl necklace and a bracelet of gold,&nbsp;&nbsp; but there were no songbirds to be&nbsp; had for love or money that winter. The lion growled with&nbsp; impatience, shaking the leaves.&nbsp;&nbsp; The merchant also shook at the idea of&nbsp; staying trapped in the tree. He thought,&nbsp;&nbsp; Perhaps it will only be my hunting dog or&nbsp; the barn cat. So he agreed to the bargain. A dozen more&nbsp;&nbsp; lions came and took their places,&nbsp; watching her with their golden eyes.&nbsp;&nbsp; The great lion circled around her, then&nbsp; stopped in front of her and waited. She showed&nbsp; no fear, but her knees trembled.&nbsp;&nbsp; Her words and courage touched the lion's heart. "Welcome to my home, lady," the&nbsp; great lion said. The lady looked&nbsp;&nbsp; into his gentle eyes and became less afraid. He led her inside the castle. One day news came that her sisters&nbsp; were to be married. The lady longed&nbsp;&nbsp; to see her family again and&nbsp; introduce her beloved husband. The prince offered to have his lions escort her. "Please, come with me," she said to&nbsp; him. Unable to deny his lady anything,&nbsp;&nbsp; the prince agreed and they set out the next day. Her family was delighted to see her. The&nbsp; lady had a room built in her father's house,&nbsp;&nbsp; with walls so thick, no light could penetrate.&nbsp; As night fell, the lion shut himself inside. But&nbsp;&nbsp; the wood of the door was green, and&nbsp; as it dried, a tiny crack appeared. The dove flew over her head and out the door,&nbsp;&nbsp; dropping a feather as he did so. The&nbsp; lady caught the feather and followed.
