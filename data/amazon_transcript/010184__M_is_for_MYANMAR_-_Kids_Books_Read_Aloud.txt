Hla, my older sister, knows&nbsp;everything about everything. What's far away? I ask her. The lights in the airplane cabin dim. "Far away&nbsp;is on the other side of earth where everyone eats soup for breakfast. Look..." Golden Pagodas Unlike anything you've ever seen - buildings&nbsp; that look like big golden bells, or dopey hats,&nbsp;&nbsp; or very pointy mountains. These are pagodas.&nbsp;&nbsp; Each one is bursting with dreams. When a person visits a pagoda they fill it up - with whale-sized dreams, snail-sized dreams, dreams of ice cream, or of floating high up into the sky. Thanaka Paste Nobody likes a sunburn. Not me, not you, not&nbsp;Htun or her sister Aye. That's why they're painting circles, squiggles, dots of thanaka&nbsp; paste on their sweet cheeks. Myanmar is so hot sometimes staying cool is the only thing that&nbsp;matters. Htun and Aye dream of jumping in a river, eating thirteen cubes of ice and drinking whole&nbsp;glasses of sugarcane juice all-in-one-gulp. Trishaw The Trishaw driver rings his bell.&nbsp;"Going east, going west? Going to school to take a test? I can peddle while you study.&nbsp;I can pedal while you eat a plate of pickled tea with a buddy. But best of all is pretending&nbsp;to float on a boat to the Irrawaddy's ending." Yangon Turn the corner and discover - buses,&nbsp;bicycles, bells, a man selling hats, and a monk beneath a maroon parasol. This is - dizzy, busy, balmy - Yangon! Terrific traffic, madness and&nbsp;sweet sago soup! But you can always look up and find a big blue&nbsp;sky open like a periwinkle yawning. Shwedagon Pagoda In the center of Yangon, Shwedagon sits - the&nbsp;grandest pagoda, with more Bama Lama than rosebuds in spring! Some say Shwedagon can make wishes come&nbsp;true. I say it shines like a hundred crazy stars. "Is the Shwedagon Pagoda made from&nbsp;real gold?" I ask. "Sixty tons of it," Hla says. “A ton weighs half as much as an&nbsp;elephant," I say. “Honestly?” Hla asks. (I can barely believe I know something my sister doesn't.) "Yes," I say. "Really?" she asks. " Yes, really," I say. Hla does the math. "That means&nbsp;Shwedagon is made from thirty elephants of gold," she says. For a moment we both say nothing at&nbsp; all, trying to imagine thirty Golden Elephants. Rice Patty Fields Can you see the boy and his two buffalo walking&nbsp;out into the rice patty fields? The boy says, "Why do birds always fly in a V? Both buffalos shake their heads left, then right. They don't know. Then&nbsp;the boy asks an easier question: "What do you think will be for dinner?" "Grass!"&nbsp;both buffalos say at the very same time. The boy looks at the two buffalos like they're&nbsp;crazy. Then the boy laughs and laughs. Longyi The young man in the purple longyi thinks&nbsp;about soccer (not homework!!!) and the latest Lu Min movie, but when he sees&nbsp;his new lady, in her new pink longyi, all his thoughts fly from his head. (He's&nbsp;almost speechless.) "Hlade," he says, You're beautiful! Later they&nbsp;dance and sing and stay up late. Look at the majestic monastery! Inside,&nbsp;many monks sit extremely still, paying very close attention to the tips of their noses. Meditation is an exercise like riding a bike, or doing multiplication tables, it helps&nbsp;make the mind aware and kind. When the monks finish meditating they wiggle their&nbsp;toeses and say a short prayer for everyone in the entire world to share: "Amhya...&nbsp;amhya... amhya... sahdu... sahdu...sahdu." Bamboo Grove The bamboo grove is rippling,&nbsp;quivering - green, green,&nbsp;green! Listen to the wind in the leaves&nbsp; -- sishussssssishussssssssishusssssssss. That means: "raise your arms above your&nbsp;head and shake them like a wildflower dancing. You can never know what will happen&nbsp; in-a-minute, in-an-hour, in-a-day so just wiggle and giggle and sway!" The bamboo grove&nbsp;is rippling, quivering green, green, green! Inle Lake The new nosey egrets fly low over the lake and chat with the fisherman poised at the very back of his boat. "Hey Mister!" They&nbsp;say, “Why are you standing? The way you row seems strangely demanding!" The fisherman&nbsp;laughs, then tosses his big net overboard saying, “Sisters, mark my words, paddling&nbsp;with legs isn't only for birds.” "Can egrets really ask questions?" "Yes." "Do&nbsp;monks pay that much attention to their noses?" "Yes. No, well yes and no, "Hla says. "And do the fishermen in Myanmar really row standing up? "I ask. "On Inle&nbsp;Lake they do. "Hla says. "Wow. "I say, "Myanmar is so different. "Then I stop&nbsp;to ponder. In many ways Myanmar doesn't seem strange at all - people in Myanmar jump&nbsp;into rivers, get sunburns, dream big dreams, and talk about what's for dinner. "Are we&nbsp;there yet? "I ask. "No." "How much longer? "I ask. "Soon, "Hla says. "Soon." "Tell me&nbsp;more," I say. "Okay," Hla says. "Let's see..." Pwe A pwe is a big party. Everyone in the village&nbsp;comes to watch wondrous dramas about star-struck mommas. Women twirl swords&nbsp;and flowers, the drums bang and crash and quake. In the morning we walk home singing,&nbsp; hoisting our songs up into the brand new sky. Mohinga Noodle Soup (a chant to rise the sun) "Hey! Mr. Soupman stirring that pot, I hope&nbsp;it's Mohinga you've got. I have a hankering, a craving, I'm hungry, I'm raving - I need noodles! Pronto! Finish 'em off with a fish from the river,&nbsp; lemongrass, and a dash of ginger." Golden Rock At the top of Mount Kyaiktiyo, a golden&nbsp; rock balances on a single strand of the Buddha's hair. Don't worry! Nothing in the&nbsp;entire wide world could make that rock roll. Bells (a haiku) Open any door and hear hundreds of bells,&nbsp;bells, bells, bells, bells ringing! Water Festival Just before the monsoons begin - when the rain falls in buckets! in boatloads! headily, steadily for a month or three, making it a seriously wishy-washy season - we Burmese celebrate the Buddhist New Year. People spray water everywhere and on everyone,&nbsp;washing away last year's woes and stubbed toes,&nbsp;&nbsp; all of its fizzles and flops. This way&nbsp;each year can begin anew, fresh and&nbsp;full of only good things like: mangoes,&nbsp;fireworks, friends, and big adventures. The plane bumps along the runway. "We made&nbsp;it!" I say. "Ready?" Hla says, and she takes my by the hand. We walk down the plane's stairs into a&nbsp;country that I have never been to before. I am so curious. I wonder if someone is going to stroll&nbsp;by and dump a big bowl of water on me. I wonder if I will get to wear thanaka paste and talk with&nbsp;a buffalo. "It's so good to be back," Hla says. She was born in Myanmar, that's why she knows&nbsp;much. "Mingalaba!" a stranger calls out -- Hello.
