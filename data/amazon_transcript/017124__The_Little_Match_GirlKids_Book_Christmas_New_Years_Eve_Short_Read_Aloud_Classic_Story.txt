First, I notice how powerful the story is,&nbsp;&nbsp; and how it touches my emotions when I read it.&nbsp; I think the writer Hans Christian Andersen and&nbsp;&nbsp; the artist Jerry Pinkney intended it that&nbsp; way. They wanted to touch our emotions,&nbsp;&nbsp; to make us feel strong feelings when we&nbsp; read it. So if that happens to you when&nbsp;&nbsp; you are reading a story--or watching a movie,&nbsp; or listening to a song--that's a good thing. Let those feelings come up, even if&nbsp; they are sad feelings. It's healthy&nbsp;&nbsp; to be sad sometimes. A full life is one&nbsp; that includes all kinds of emotions,&nbsp;&nbsp; and an artwork, like this story,&nbsp; can help open those doors for us. I also notice on the very first page of the story,&nbsp; the illustration shows the girl making artificial&nbsp;&nbsp; flowers along with her brothers and sisters&nbsp; at the table while their father watches them. The children seem nervous and quiet,&nbsp; even afraid of their father. I could&nbsp;&nbsp; tell right away this is not a happy&nbsp; family. Later in the story we find out&nbsp;&nbsp; the father beats the children if they&nbsp; come home without selling anything. And then we see how poor her family is, and how&nbsp; she has no shoes of her own, and the little girl&nbsp;&nbsp; has a job, working in the dangerous streets.&nbsp; She has such a hard life. She reminds me of&nbsp;&nbsp; Cinderella, who also had an abusive family, and&nbsp; Hansel and Gretel, who also faced starvation.&nbsp;&nbsp; Storybooks can show us the hard truth about life,&nbsp; which sometimes includes pain and suffering. And the hardest truth in this story comes at the&nbsp; end, when the little girl freezes to death. Things&nbsp;&nbsp; like this do sometimes happen in real life. If&nbsp; there is comfort in the story, it's that the&nbsp;&nbsp; little girl, facing an impossible situation, uses&nbsp; her imagination to dream of happier circumstances,&nbsp;&nbsp; of having everything she wants. At the most&nbsp; difficult time, in the girl's final moments,&nbsp;&nbsp; she thinks of her grandmother, the person who&nbsp; loved her most of all. This vision of being&nbsp;&nbsp; held by her grandmother, of being surrounded by&nbsp; love, and going away to a place with no more pain,&nbsp;&nbsp; is what takes the terrible sadness of this&nbsp; story and makes something beautiful from it. I have no wish to suffer like our main character&nbsp; does, and I certainly don't want to die the way&nbsp;&nbsp; that she does, alone in the freezing cold and&nbsp; darkness. Even so, all of us will die someday, and&nbsp;&nbsp; when it's my turn, I do hope that--like the little&nbsp; match girl--my last thought is that I am loved.
