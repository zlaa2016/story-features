The festivities went on and on for days and days,&nbsp;&nbsp; and if they haven't grown tired of all&nbsp; their merry-making, it goes on still. What do we notice about this story? First, I notice that when Tom's father,&nbsp;&nbsp; Tall Tim of the Mountains, tells&nbsp; his wife that he'd be happy even if his child were no bigger than his thumb,&nbsp;&nbsp; it's a lovely way for a father to&nbsp; express the nature of his love— that it doesn't matter what&nbsp; size his child would be. Yet when Tall Tim uses those&nbsp; same words with Merlin,&nbsp;&nbsp; he is giving the mischievous old wizard an idea, and Merlin grants him exactly what Tall Tim asked. This reminds me of the old saying, "Be careful&nbsp; what you wish for; you just might get it." As it is, things work out well for Tom Thumb,&nbsp;&nbsp; as his unique size draws the&nbsp; attention of the Queen of the Fairies, who makes him fully grown, grants him the power&nbsp; to talk to animals, and gives him a magic sword. Again and again throughout the story, where his&nbsp; tiny size would seem to make life harder for him, instead it works to his advantage, in whispering&nbsp; to the plough horse, or in facing the giant Gembo, who has already defeated all of&nbsp; King Arthur's bravest knights. What Tom lacks in size and physical&nbsp; power, he makes up for in confidence, and cleverness, and his&nbsp; ability to tell a good story. All of these are qualities&nbsp; that help him to be a success. Speaking of strength, the story shows us&nbsp; there is more than one way of being strong. I was surprised to notice how&nbsp; Gembo, so big and so cruel,&nbsp;&nbsp; was defeated not by Tom Thumb's&nbsp; strength, but by his own weakness. I don't mean physical weakness. Of course&nbsp; as a giant he was physically strong. I mean Gembo's weakness of character-- he had too much pride. Gembo cared so much about&nbsp; what other people might think,&nbsp;&nbsp; and was so embarrassed by&nbsp; the idea of being laughed at, that he left the castle and went away, after&nbsp; Tom Thumb got the best of him just once. Gembo doesn't know how to handle it when things&nbsp; don't go his way. We might say he is a sore loser. The story shows us that the smallest&nbsp; person can have a strong character;&nbsp;&nbsp; it also shows us that the biggest&nbsp; person can have a weak character. Those who have strong character, like Tom Thumb,&nbsp; can overcome the problems life presents them, while those who have weak character, like&nbsp; Gembo, are crushed by the smallest setback.
