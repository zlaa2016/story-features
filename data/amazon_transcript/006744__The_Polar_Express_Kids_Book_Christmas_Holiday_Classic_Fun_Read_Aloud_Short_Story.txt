Today we'll be reading "The Polar Express." "The Polar Express," written and&nbsp; illustrated by Chris Van Allsburg On Christmas Eve, many years&nbsp; ago, I lay quietly in my bed. I did not rustle the sheets. I breathed slowly and silently. I was listening for a sound—a sound&nbsp; a friend had told me I'd never hear— the ringing bells of Santa's sleigh. He took a large pocket watch from his vest,&nbsp;&nbsp; then looked up at my window. I put on my slippers&nbsp; and robe. I tiptoed downstairs and out the door. "This is the Polar Express." I took his outstretched hand&nbsp; and he pulled me aboard. The train was filled with other children,&nbsp; all in their pajamas and nightgowns. We sang Christmas carols and ate candies&nbsp; with nougat centers as white as snow. We drank hot cocoa as thick and&nbsp; rich as melted chocolate bars. "They are gathering at the center&nbsp; of the city," the conductor told us.&nbsp;&nbsp; "That is where Santa will give&nbsp; the first gift of Christmas." "Who receives the first gift?" we all asked. The conductor answered, "He will choose one of you." "Look," shouted one of the children, "the elves." Outside we saw hundreds of elves. As our train&nbsp; drew closer to the center of the North Pole,&nbsp;&nbsp; we slowed to a crawl, so crowded were&nbsp; the streets with Santa's helpers. When the Polar Express could go no farther,&nbsp; we stopped and the conductor led us outside. We pressed through the crowd to&nbsp; the edge of a large, open circle. In front of us stood Santa's sleigh. The&nbsp; reindeer were excited. They pranced and paced,&nbsp;&nbsp; ringing the silver sleigh bells that hung&nbsp; from their harnesses. It was a magical sound,&nbsp;&nbsp; like nothing I'd ever heard. Across the circle,&nbsp;&nbsp; the elves moved apart and Santa Claus&nbsp; appeared. The elves cheered wildly. He marched over to us and, pointing to&nbsp; me, said, "Let's have this fellow here." He jumped into his sleigh.&nbsp; The conductor handed me up. I sat on Santa's knee and he asked,&nbsp; "Now, what would you like for Christmas?" I knew that I could have any gift I could imagine. But the thing I wanted most for Christmas&nbsp; was not inside Santa's giant bag. What I wanted more than anything was&nbsp; one silver bell from Santa's sleigh. When I asked, Santa smiled. Then he gave me a hug and told an elf&nbsp; to cut a bell from a reindeer's harness. The elf tossed it up to Santa. He stood, holding the bell high above him,&nbsp; and called out, "The first gift of Christmas!" A clock struck midnight as the elves roared&nbsp; their approval. Santa handed the bell to me,&nbsp;&nbsp; and I put it in my bathrobe pocket. The&nbsp; conductor helped me down from the sleigh. Santa shouted out the reindeer's&nbsp; names and cracked his whip. His team charged forward and climbed into&nbsp; the air. Santa circled once above us,&nbsp;&nbsp; then disappeared in the cold, dark polar sky. As soon as we were back inside the Polar Express,&nbsp;&nbsp; the other children asked to see&nbsp; the bell. I reached into my pocket,&nbsp;&nbsp; but the only thing I felt was a hole. I had&nbsp; lost the silver bell from Santa Claus's sleigh. "Let's hurry outside and look for&nbsp; it," one of the children said. But the train gave a sudden lurch and&nbsp; started moving. We were on our way home. It broke my heart to lose the bell.&nbsp; When the train reached my house,&nbsp;&nbsp; I sadly left the other children. I&nbsp; stood at my doorway and waved good-bye. The conductor said something from the&nbsp; moving train, but I couldn't hear him. "What?" I yelled out. He cupped his hands around his mouth. "MERRY CHRISTMAS," he shouted. The Polar Express let out a loud&nbsp; blast from its whistle and sped away. On Christmas morning my little sister&nbsp; Sarah and I opened our presents. When it looked as if&nbsp; everything had been unwrapped, Sarah found one last small box behind the tree. It had my name on it. Inside was the silver bell! There was a note: "Found this on the seat of my sleigh. Fix that hole in your pocket." Signed, "Mr. C." I shook the bell. It made the most beautiful&nbsp; sound my sister and I had ever heard. But my mother said, "Oh, that's too bad." "Yes," said my father, "it's broken." When I'd shaken the bell, my&nbsp; parents had not heard a sound. At one time most of my friends could&nbsp; hear the bell, but as years passed,&nbsp;&nbsp; it fell silent for all of them. Even Sarah found one Christmas that she&nbsp; could no longer hear its sweet sound.&nbsp;&nbsp; Though I've grown old, the bell still rings&nbsp; for me as it does for all who truly believe.
