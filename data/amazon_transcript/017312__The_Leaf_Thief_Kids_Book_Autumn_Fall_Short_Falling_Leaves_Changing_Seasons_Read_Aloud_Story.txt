But there is more to it than that.&nbsp; The wind can only blow the leaves&nbsp;&nbsp; away when the trees have started to shed&nbsp; their leaves. This happens in the autumn&nbsp;&nbsp; when the temperature drops, marking&nbsp; the change from summer to winter. Trees look very pretty in the autumn. Before the leaves fall, they turn from&nbsp; green to all sorts of different colors. "Red, gold, orange..." When they turn brown, the leaves&nbsp; are ready to fall from the tree. Not all trees shed their leaves. Only deciduous trees lose their leaves.&nbsp; Coniferous trees, such as evergreens, keep theirs. Autumn happens at different&nbsp; times across the world. In the Northern Hemisphere,&nbsp; autumn starts in September.&nbsp;&nbsp; In the Southern Hemisphere, it starts in March. Autumn isn't just about falling leaves. Other changes happen too. The&nbsp; daylight hours shorten and some&nbsp;&nbsp; birds and butterflies fly to&nbsp; warmer climates, or migrate. Other animals like bats and bears&nbsp; sleep through the winter, or hibernate. Squirrels don't hibernate but&nbsp; they do begin to sleep a lot more. They also store nuts and other&nbsp; food for the colder months ahead.
