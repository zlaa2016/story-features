Merlin and the Making of the King,&nbsp; retold by Margaret Hodges, from Sir&nbsp;&nbsp; Thomas Malory's Le Morte d'Arthur,&nbsp; illustrated by Trina Schart Hyman The Sword in the Stone In the days before Arthur was born, Merlin the&nbsp; magician hid himself in a cave that faced the&nbsp;&nbsp; western sea. You do not know how&nbsp; to live at peace, and you have not long to live.&nbsp;&nbsp; When Igraine bears a son, I will wait&nbsp; at the postern gate of your castle to&nbsp;&nbsp; carry the child to safety. Now that is not to&nbsp; be. But your son shall rule and all the&nbsp;&nbsp; world shall know of King Arthur's knights,&nbsp; who will sit with him at his Round Table." Uther believed Merlin's words.&nbsp; When Igraine's baby was born,&nbsp;&nbsp; she wrapped him in cloth of gold and&nbsp; carried him by night to the postern gate.&nbsp;&nbsp; There with tears she gave him to Merlin, who&nbsp; covered him with his cloak and disappeared. Uther&nbsp;&nbsp; found the great table in Merlin's cave and gave&nbsp; it for safekeeping to a faithful friend and ally. Not long afterward Uther&nbsp; died, as Merlin had foreseen.&nbsp;&nbsp; But Arthur was safe. Merlin had taken him to the&nbsp; house of a good man, Sir Ector, whose wife nursed&nbsp;&nbsp; the baby as if he had been her own child. He grew&nbsp; up learning all that a knight must do and be.&nbsp;&nbsp; Arthur loved Sir Ector and his wife and their&nbsp; son, Sir Kay, who had just been knighted.&nbsp;&nbsp; Kay was like a brother to Arthur,&nbsp; but secretly he wanted to be king. When no leader could win enough battles to be&nbsp; named king, Merlin saw that Arthur's time had&nbsp;&nbsp; come. By magic, at Christmastime, he set a heavy&nbsp; square stone in front of London's greatest church.&nbsp;&nbsp; On this stone stood an iron anvil and into&nbsp; the anvil and stone a naked sword was thrust.&nbsp;&nbsp; On the blade of the sword were letters of gold: WHOSO PULLETH OUT THIS SWORD OF THIS STONE AND&nbsp; ANVIL IS RIGHTWISE KING BORN OF ALL ENGLAND. As nobles and knights stared at the strange&nbsp; sight, Merlin appeared and spoke to them:&nbsp;&nbsp; "Through this sword, God will work a&nbsp; miracle to show who shall be king." One by one, they tried with might and main, but&nbsp; none could move the sword even a hairsbreadth. "Have a tournament until a sign comes&nbsp; from heaven," said Merlin, and all agreed. "But I forgot my sword," said Kay. "Arthur, go&nbsp; to my father's lodging and bring me a sword." Arthur rode off willingly, but&nbsp; found the house locked and empty.&nbsp;&nbsp; Then he remembered the sword in the&nbsp; stone. He rode back to the churchyard.&nbsp;&nbsp; Everyone had gone to the tournament,&nbsp; and he found the sword untouched.&nbsp;&nbsp; He did not see Merlin standing in the shadows.&nbsp; Lightly Arthur took the sword by the hilt and&nbsp;&nbsp; smoothly he pulled it out of the stone. Then he&nbsp; found Sir Kay and said, "Here is a sword for you." Amazed, Kay took it. He galloped&nbsp; off to his father and cried,&nbsp;&nbsp; "I have the sword of the stone! I must be king!" But Sir Ector said, "Tell me on your&nbsp; oath, how did you get the sword?" Kay was ashamed. "Arthur&nbsp; brought it to me," he said. Sir Ector fell to his knees before Arthur, saying,&nbsp;&nbsp; "Now I know that Merlin spoke the truth&nbsp; when he brought you to my house as a baby.&nbsp;&nbsp; You are the son of Uther Pendragon,&nbsp; and you are the rightful king." Then in the sight of all the great lords, Arthur&nbsp; thrust the sword into the stone and pulled it&nbsp;&nbsp; out easily. And though he alone could succeed in&nbsp; the marvelous feat, the lords would not have him. "How can that unknown, beardless&nbsp; boy be king?" they muttered. At last the common people came and&nbsp; saw for themselves the miracle of&nbsp;&nbsp; the sword pulled from the stone by Arthur's hands. "We will have Arthur for our king!"&nbsp; they shouted. "It is God's will!" Then Arthur laid his sword on the church altar and&nbsp; knelt all night to pray that he might be worthy.&nbsp;&nbsp; He swore to live a pure life, to tell the&nbsp; truth, and to right wrongs wherever he saw them.&nbsp;&nbsp; To Arthur Merlin spoke a final word: "Never use your sword unless you&nbsp; must. He saw that&nbsp; Arthur was worthy and loved him like a son. Arthur wanted to return to Camelot.&nbsp; "But I have no sword, he said to Merlin. "You shall have a better sword,"&nbsp; said Merlin. "Come with me."
