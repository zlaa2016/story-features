Amy Wu and the Perfect Bao by Kat&nbsp; Zhang, illustrated by Charlene Chua Amy can do a lot of things. She can&nbsp; brush her teeth. She can tie her shoe. She can even do both at once... sort of. But there's one thing Amy cannot, cannot&nbsp; do. She cannot make THE PERFECT BAO. Sometimes they come out too small.&nbsp; Sometimes they come out too big. Sometimes she adds too much filling.&nbsp;&nbsp; Sometimes not enough. And sometimes they&nbsp; fall apart before they reach her mouth. Amy's mom and dad make perfect bao.&nbsp;&nbsp; So does her grandma. Their bao are&nbsp; soft and fluffy and so, so delicious. Amy could eat them all day. (Sometimes she does.) Today, Amy is going to do it—&nbsp;&nbsp; she's going to make the world's most perfect&nbsp; bao. Bao making is an all-day event. Amy's dad starts in the morning, mixing&nbsp; together the ingredients for the dough. Then it's time to knead, knead, knead.&nbsp;&nbsp; He pushes the dough! He punches&nbsp; the dough! Amy gives it a try too. They leave the dough to rise. Amy&nbsp; keeps an eye on it, just in case.&nbsp;&nbsp; It grows bigger... and bigger... and even bigger. Amy's dad squishes the dough down just in time.&nbsp; He rolls it into a log and cuts it into pieces. Meanwhile, Amy's mom seasons meat for the filling. Garlic, Mushrooms, Pepper, Salt, Ginger. Everyone gathers around the&nbsp; table and rolls up their sleeves.&nbsp;&nbsp; It's time to get to work! Amy's first bao&nbsp; turns out a little funny. So does the second. It's hard to know how much filling to add.&nbsp;&nbsp; Too little and the bao is sad&nbsp; and empty. Too much and—oops! It's also hard to pinch the bao shut&nbsp; just right. Pinch! Pinch! Pinch! Amy watches her mom make a perfect bao.&nbsp; She watches her dad make a perfect bao—&nbsp;&nbsp; and her grandma, too. They all try to teach her. "Roll out the dough like this," says Amy's dad. "Use just enough filling," says Amy's mom. "Pinch, pinch, pinch!" says Amy's grandma. But Amy's bao just aren't the same.&nbsp;&nbsp; They are too empty or too fat.&nbsp; They have holes in them. They leak. Maybe today won't be the day after all.&nbsp; Maybe Amy just can't make a perfect bao. Then Amy has an idea. The pieces of&nbsp; dough were cut for grown-up hands.&nbsp;&nbsp; But Amy's hands are very small. She&nbsp; whispers her idea into her grandma's ear. Amy's grandma cuts each piece of dough&nbsp; into two smaller pieces. Amy-size pieces.&nbsp;&nbsp; Now they fit perfectly in Amy's palms. Carefully Amy rolls the dough so it's thicker&nbsp; on the inside and thinner at the edges.&nbsp;&nbsp; She adds just the right amount of filling.&nbsp; She pinch, pinch, pinches it shut. And there it is! AMY'S PERFECT BAO! She makes another. And another. And even&nbsp; more after that. She's a bao-making master! Soon all the dough and filling are gone.&nbsp; Everyone is tired, but they're not done yet.&nbsp;&nbsp; Amy's grandma boils a big pot of&nbsp; water. It's time to steam the bao. Amy keeps an eye on the steamer, just in case.&nbsp;&nbsp; All her perfect bao—and all the&nbsp; imperfect ones too—are snug inside. The bao are done! Amy's mom&nbsp; lifts the lid off the steamer. Whoosh! Out comes a puff of steam.&nbsp; Amy can't see anything at all. The steam clears. There are Amy's perfect bao.&nbsp; They are not too small. They are not too big.&nbsp;&nbsp; They have just the right amount&nbsp; of filling, and they do not leak.&nbsp;&nbsp; They are soft and fluffy and so, so delicious. Amy eats one, then another.&nbsp;&nbsp; Then she eats one of the not-so-perfect bao.&nbsp; And you know what? It tastes just as good.
