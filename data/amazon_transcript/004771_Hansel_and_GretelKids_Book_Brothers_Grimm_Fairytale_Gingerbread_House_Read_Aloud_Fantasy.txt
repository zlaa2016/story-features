It may be a little different&nbsp; from another version you've heard before. At the edge of a great forest there once lived a&nbsp; poor woodcutter. He could scarcely manage to feed&nbsp;&nbsp; his wife and his two children, Hansel and Gretel,&nbsp; and this made him miserable. For a long time the man could&nbsp;&nbsp; not reconcile himself to his wife's plan, but she&nbsp; would give him no peace until he finally agreed. The children had heard everything their mother&nbsp; had said, and Gretel began to cry. "Hush," Hansel&nbsp;&nbsp; whispered. "It will be all right. I have an&nbsp; idea." Then he got up as quietly as he could,&nbsp;&nbsp; put on his jacket, and went outside. White pebbles&nbsp; on the ground glistened in the moonlight. Hansel&nbsp;&nbsp; carefully gathered them up and stuffed the&nbsp; pocket of his jacket with as many as would&nbsp;&nbsp; go in. Then he went back inside, lay down&nbsp; beside his little sister, and fell asleep. Early in the morning, before the sun had&nbsp; risen, the parents came and awakened the&nbsp;&nbsp; children. Early in the morning, before daybreak, they&nbsp; all got up, and each of the children was&nbsp;&nbsp; given a small piece of bread. As they walked&nbsp; along, Hansel often paused and gazed back.
