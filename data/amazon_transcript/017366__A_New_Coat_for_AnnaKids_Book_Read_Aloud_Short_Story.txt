Today we'll be reading A New Coat for&nbsp; Anna by Harriet Ziefert and Anita Lobel. A New Coat for Anna by Harriet&nbsp; Ziefert, pictures by Anita Lobel. Winter had come and Anna needed a new coat.&nbsp;&nbsp; The fuzzy blue coat that she had worn for so many&nbsp; winters was no longer fuzzy and it was very small. Last winter Anna's mother had said,&nbsp;&nbsp; "When the war is over, we will be able to buy&nbsp; things again and I will get you a nice new coat." But when the war ended the stores&nbsp; remained empty. There still were no coats.&nbsp;&nbsp; There was hardly any food.&nbsp; And no one had any money. Anna's mother wondered how she could get&nbsp; Anna a new coat. Then she had an idea. "Anna, I have no money," she said,&nbsp;&nbsp; "but I still have grandfather's gold&nbsp; watch and some other nice things. Maybe we can use them to get&nbsp; what we need for a new coat.&nbsp;&nbsp; First we need wool. Tomorrow we will visit&nbsp; a farmer and see about getting some." The next day Anna and her&nbsp; mother walked to a nearby farm. "Anna needs a new coat," Anna's mother told&nbsp; the farmer. "I have no money, but I will give&nbsp;&nbsp; you this fine gold watch if you will give me&nbsp; enough wool from your sheep to make a coat." The farmer said, "What a good idea! But&nbsp; you will have to wait until spring when&nbsp;&nbsp; I shear my sheep's winter wool. Then I can&nbsp; trade you their wool for your gold watch." Anna waited for spring to come. Almost every&nbsp; Sunday she and her mother visited the sheep.&nbsp;&nbsp; She would always ask them, "Is your wool growing?" The sheep would always answer, "Baaa!" Then she would feed them nice&nbsp; fresh hay and give them hugs. At Christmas time Anna brought them paper&nbsp; necklaces and apples and sang carols. When spring came the farmer&nbsp; sheared the sheep's wool. "Does it hurt them? asked Anna. "No, Anna," said the farmer. "It&nbsp; is just like getting a haircut." When he had enough wool to make a coat, the&nbsp; farmer showed Anna how to card the wool. "It's like untangling the knots&nbsp; in your hair," he told Anna. Then he gave Anna's mother a big bag of wool&nbsp; and Anna's mother gave him the gold watch. Anna and her mother took the bag of wool&nbsp; to an old woman who had a spinning wheel. "Anna needs a new coat," Anna's mother&nbsp; told the woman. "I have no money,&nbsp;&nbsp; but I will give you this beautiful lamp&nbsp; if you will spin this wool into yarn." The woman said, "A lamp. That's just what I&nbsp; need. But I cannot spin quickly for I am old&nbsp;&nbsp; and my fingers are stiff. Come back when the&nbsp; cherries are ripe and I will have your yarn." When summer came, Anna and her mother&nbsp; returned. Anna's mother gave the old&nbsp;&nbsp; woman the lamp and the old woman gave them the&nbsp; yarn and a basket of delicious red cherries. "Anna, what color coat would&nbsp; you like?" Anna's mother asked. "A red one!" Anna answered. "Then we will pick some lingonberries," said&nbsp; Anna's mother. "They make a beautiful red dye." At the end of summer, Anna's mother knew just the&nbsp;&nbsp; place in the woods to find&nbsp; the ripest lingonberries. Anna and her mother boiled water in a&nbsp; big pot and put the berries into it.&nbsp;&nbsp; The water turned a deep red. Anna's&nbsp; mother dipped the pale yarn into it. Soon red yarn was hanging up to dry&nbsp; on a clothesline strung across the&nbsp;&nbsp; kitchen. When it dried, Anna and her&nbsp; mother wound the yarn into balls. They took the yarn to the weaver. "Anna needs a new coat," Anna's&nbsp; mother said. "I have no money,&nbsp;&nbsp; but I will give you this garnet necklace&nbsp; if you will weave this yarn into cloth." The weaver said, "What a pretty necklace.&nbsp;&nbsp; I will be happy to weave your&nbsp; yarn. Come back in two weeks." When Anna and her mother returned, the weaver&nbsp; gave them a bolt of beautiful red cloth.&nbsp;&nbsp; Anna's mother gave the weaver&nbsp; the sparkling garnet necklace. The next day Anna and her mother&nbsp; set off to see the tailor. "Winter is coming and Anna needs a new coat,"&nbsp; Anna's mother told the tailor. "I have no money,&nbsp;&nbsp; but I will give you this porcelain teapot&nbsp; if you will make a coat from this cloth." The tailor said, "That's a pretty teapot.&nbsp;&nbsp; Anna, I'd be very happy to make you a new&nbsp; coat, but first I must take your measurements." He measured her shoulders. He measured her arms. He measured from the back of her&nbsp; neck to the back of her knees.&nbsp;&nbsp; Then he said, "Come back next&nbsp; week and I will have your coat." The tailor set to work making a pattern,&nbsp;&nbsp; cutting the cloth, pinning, and&nbsp; sewing and stitching and snipping. He worked and worked for almost a&nbsp; whole week. When he was finished,&nbsp;&nbsp; he found six pretty matching buttons in&nbsp; his button box and sewed them on the coat. He hung the coat proudly in&nbsp; the window for everyone to see. When Anna and her mother&nbsp; returned to the tailor's shop,&nbsp;&nbsp; Anna tried on her new coat. She twirled around&nbsp; in front of the mirror. The coat was perfect! Anna thanked the tailor.&nbsp; Anna's mother thanked him, too,&nbsp;&nbsp; and gave him the pretty porcelain teapot. Anna wore her new coat home. She stopped at every&nbsp; store to look at her reflection in the window. When they got home her mother&nbsp; said, "Christmas will soon be here,&nbsp;&nbsp; and I think this year we could&nbsp; have a little celebration." Anna said, "Oh, yes, and please could we invite&nbsp; all the people who helped to make my coat?" "Yes," said Anna's mother. "And I will&nbsp; make a Christmas cake just like I used to." Anna gave her mother a big hug. On Christmas Eve the farmer, the spinner, the&nbsp; weaver, and the tailor came to Anna's house.&nbsp;&nbsp; They all thought Anna looked&nbsp; beautiful in her new coat. The Christmas cake that Anna's&nbsp; mother baked was delicious.&nbsp;&nbsp; Everyone agreed that this was the best&nbsp; Christmas they had had in a long time. On Christmas Day Anna visited the sheep.&nbsp;&nbsp; "Thank you for the wool, sheep," she&nbsp; said. "Do you like my pretty new coat?" The sheep seemed to smile as&nbsp; they answered, "Baaa! Baaa!"
