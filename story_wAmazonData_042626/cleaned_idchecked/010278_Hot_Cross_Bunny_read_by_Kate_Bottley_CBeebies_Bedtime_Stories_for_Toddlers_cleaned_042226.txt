Our story begins where the sun's always sunny, on the top-secret isle of the Great Easter Bunny. It starts bright and early one fair Easter eve, with a very small, very cross rabbit called Steve. The Easter egg harvest was well underway, but Steve wasn't excited for this Easter day. A real hot cross bunny from ear tufts to bum, for he'd not grown a chocolate egg all year, not one. "It's hopeless," Steve grumbled. His heart felt so squeezy. "This chocolate egg growing is meant to be easy." "Relax," replied Nugget. "Don't stress yourself out." "Are chocolate eggs all Easter day is about?" "Yes," said Steve crossly. "I cannot give up. I want to win this year's Golden Egg Cup. I've followed my books like the instructions said, but what if I make up my own way instead?" Into his pot went no sugar but spice, dark chocolate chips, fiery chilies, and ice. Two lemons, one earthworm, six sharp pointy rocks, ten daisy flowers, three old spotty socks. Then his last jelly bean, the most important part, for the best Easter eggs have a jelly bean heart. A silver mist swirled and the moon glittered blue. At the last stroke of midnight, Steve's special egg grew. "It's eggnormous!" cried Nugget. "Eggtastic! A dream! It's the eggiest egg I think I've ever seen." And that's when they heard it—a crack, then a scratch. Steve's egg experiment hiccuped, then started to hatch. And out of the crack, with a thunderous croak, a snarl, and a sniff, and a cloud of gray smoke, hatched a huge hungry dragon a shocking surprise. The friends wept as it took to the skies. It soared across the moon, then it swished and it swirled, and it plunged through the rabbit hole to the real world. "We must catch it!" Steve cried. "Nugget, flat out, you're mad! We can't catch a dragon. They're badder than bad. We'll be roasted and toasted and barbecued too." But Nugget, Steve said, "It's the right thing to do." So they hopped into action despite Nugget's warning, to catch the loose dragon before Easter morning. It flew over rooftops, it leaped over cars, swam in a fish pond, it sprang for the stars. It bounced around town and then, no, one by one, it ate every chocolate egg till there were none. Steve felt so hopless. It was nearly dawn. Soon children would wake up, they'd stretch and they'd yawn. They'd hunt high and low, but they'd find no egg hid, not a single egg left for the most hopful kid. The dragon, despite feeling really quite sick, swooped toward a house and with one swirling flick, squashed down the chimney in search of a treat, because something inside smelled delicious to eat. Steve and Nugget tiptoed through the dark sleeping house and sneaked up to the dragon as quiet as a mouse. "Don't eat us," Steve cried just as Nugget squeaked, "Help!" And the terrified dragon leaped up with a yelp. Poor Trevor the dragon was friendly, not mean, because Trevor's sweetheart was a peach jelly bean. Then to their surprise, with a sound like a drum, hundreds of chocolate eggs burped from Trev's bum. "It's chocolate!" Steve sprang up. "I know what to do. We'll replace the lost eggs with the chocolate egg poo." He surfed from the house on a loose roller skate, crashed through the door and the small garden gate. They raced around town, every poo egg was hid, to delight and excite every egg-hunting kid. With Easter day saved and the sun coming up, it was time to see who'd won the Golden Egg Cup. Back home after such an eggstraordinary night, the Great Easter Bunny bestowed with delight the Golden Egg Cup for the greatest egg ever to brave Steve and Nugget and their new pal Trevor. With the sun shining bright, the egg hunts underway, Steve realised the best thing about Easter Day wasn't the chocolate or the prize in the end, but to share joy and laughter and make a new friend. Well done, Steve. Well done, Nugget. And well done, Trevor. You all helped make it the best Easter yet. You more than deserve that Golden Egg Cup. But after all that egg-citement, it must be time for bed. I'm egghausted, aren't you? So have hoppy dreams and I'll see you soon for another bedtime story. Night night now.
