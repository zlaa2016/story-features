Play (a little.) Homework (uggg.) Purple polka dot pajamas and back to bed. Bruno was feeling blue. So Bruno did what&nbsp;he always did when he needed time to think. Bruno walked down to the sea. Something in&nbsp;the distance moved. One tentacle appeared. Then two. Then four. Then eight. "Hello,&nbsp;said the octopus. "Go away," said Bruno. "Your malady is common." The starry-eyed&nbsp;cephalopod said. "You've lost your wonder, That's all. But I know where to find it." Oh, yeah. Where? Agus placed a suction cup on Bruno's wing and said in a very wise&nbsp;voice, "The world is big and round and full of adventures. Let's go to..." Indonesia "Go!" said a little beach critter. Bruno jumped into the sea. Sometimes the waves&nbsp;were wild, but Bruno and Agus swam on. They saw weird fishes and waxed&nbsp; philosophic about the meaning of life. Where do parrots go in a monsoon? And why do clouds occasionally cry? Days passed. All of a sudden a big beast rose out of the&nbsp;sea. "Looking for wonder? For a mystery and thunder? Swim on you adventurers. Indonesia is near." A volcano erupted in the distance. "Krakatoa!"Agus said. "We made it!" A striped tapir lounged on the shore. "Selamat," the tapir hissed. "That means hello," Agus said. The air was warm. The palm trees swayed. Bruno heard whimsical, watery&nbsp; sounds. "Am I dreaming?" Bruno wondered. "No," Agus said. "You are in Indonesia&nbsp;now, and that, my friend, is Gamelan music. Gama-what? Agus laughed. The music played on.&nbsp; "Follow that noise!" He called over his shoulder. Bruno and Agus swam from Aceh (where the trees are&nbsp;as big as school buses) to Java (like the coffee) and down to Bali to bask in the sun. There are&nbsp;more than 17,000 islands in Indonesia. Imagine that! In Moluccas they hunted the blue-feathered&nbsp;cassowary, the deadliest bird in the world. "What's a cassowary look like anyways?" "I&nbsp;don't know," Agus said softly. "No one has ever lived to tell the tale." Wow, Bruno&nbsp;thought everywhere they went. "Look at that," "and that" "AND THAT!" People in Indonesia&nbsp;wear crazy colored clothes called batik. And they whisper sweet nothings&nbsp;to Buta Kala monsters. Buta Kala monsters are the biggest and&nbsp;brightest monsters on earth. Bruno stood right side up, he was so flipping&nbsp;excited. "Kamu gila," Agus said. "You're crazy but I like you," which is just about the&nbsp;best compliment an octopus can give. Bruno and Agus explored the Borobudur temple. "Borobudur is 1,300 years old," Agus explained. "It's a magical, man-made, stone mountain, a crazy cosmology, the epicenter of the earth!" Sometimes Agus sounds like&nbsp;he knows everything about everything, which he does. They visited Agus' family in Cenderawashi Bay. The place was packed. Peg-leg pirates! Squids, sunfish, cuttlefish! Technicolor dreamboats! "Listen up," Agus' mother&nbsp;said, "Have you done your homework?" Maybe Indonesia isn't so different from Florida, Bruno thought to himself. In Jakarta, Bruno and Agus got stuck in traffic FOREVER. There are 18 million people living in Jakarta. That is twice as many as New York&nbsp;City, and all of them are going someplace! The air was thick with dust and smog. Bruno&nbsp;felt crazy, Bruno felt ravey, Bruno wanted to jump back into the sea. Which is exactly&nbsp;what Bruno did. Bruno was ready to go. Somehow swimming back always seems faster&nbsp;than swimming out. Soon Bruno was home. Bruno looked around, maybe Florida was just&nbsp;as exciting and wonderful as Indonesia. The air was warm. The palm tree swayed. Bruno heard some far-away watery sounds... As Agus headed out into open water, he recited one of his&nbsp;favorite poems. "And the end of all our exploring Will be to arrive where we started And know the&nbsp;place for the first time." TS Elliot said that.
