Once upon a time there was a misty blue mountain. Below the misty blue mountain was a wild dark forest. And by the wild dark forest was a village. The village had a stream and a duck pond and old red apple tree. And it was home to Jack and his fairy tale friends. Jack loved climbing. He was always climbing the pine tree near his friend's beauty's house and the old red apple tree in Griselda's garden now. Don't go climbing our boontocks. warned Jack's mother. It was spring and their seedlings were starting to grow. The socks will break and we'll have no beans to make bean stew for the village festival. I won't, mom, Jack promised. But that summer, the beantocks grew taller and taller and taller until they were higher than Jack's house. Jack longed to climb them. "I wonder how far you can see from the top," he said to Cinderella as they were walking home from school. One night, Jack couldn't resist any longer. He crept out of the house and started to climb the nearest stock. Creek, creek, swayed the stalk as Jack climbed through the leaves. Then, snap! The stalk broke and Jack tumbled down. But Jack couldn't resist trying again. Surely he'd reach the top next time. So he started to climb the next stop. Snap. Then the next snap. Then the next snap. until all the beantocks were broken. Jack went to bed feeling awful. What was he going to tell his mother? Something terrible has happened to our beantocks, cried Jack's mom the following morning. Did you climb them, Jack? No, lied Jack. Tell me the truth, Jack. Was it you? No, lied Jack again. Remember, I've always taught you to be honest, said Jack's mom. Did you break the bean socks, Jack? No, like Jack for a third time. It must have been Gazela's cat. Puss. Jack ran away and hid in Griselda's apple tree. "Whatever are you doing up there?" called Rela. I lied to my mom. I told her that puss broke our brain socks, but it was me. Oh dear, said Griselda. Don't you think you should tell her the truth? Jack nodded. He knew it was the right thing to do. But I don't know what to do about our beantock, said Jack. We have no beans now to make bean stew. Brazilla gave Jack a big purple bean. Try planting this. It was me, Mom, said Jack when he got home. I'm sorry I broke the beads stops and I'm sorry I lied. Oh, Jack, I knew it was you, replied his mom. I've been hoping and waiting for you to tell me the truth. Are you mad? asked Jack. Not now you're being honest, said his mom, giving him a hug. Look what Griselda gave me," Jack said, showing his mom the bean. Jack and his mom planted Griselda's purple bean in the garden that night. The next day, they had a big surprise. The bean had grown like magic into a huge leafy bean sock with a thick, strong stem that reached all the way to the clouds. "Just look at those lovely beans," said Jack's mother. "How are we going to pick them?" asked Jack. "I think you're going to have to climb the beantock," laughed his mom. So Jack got to climb the top to the top of the beantock after all. And he picked so many beans he and his mother were able to make lots of lovely bean stew for the village festival. The end.
