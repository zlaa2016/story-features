High in a tall gum tree where the silver leaves shimmered in the morning light lived a curious young magpie named Max. His feathers were sleek black and crisp white, shining like polished stones. He loved two things most of all, finding treasures and discovering new tastes. One bright morning, Max soared low over the grass. The dew still clung to the blades, glittering like little diamonds. Suddenly, a strange smell drifted up on the breeze. It was salty, strong, and tickled his beak. Max swooped closer, and beneath a tall gum tree on a forgotten picnic rug, lay a Vegemite sandwich. The bread was soft and covered in golden butter and a dark sticky layer that peaked out between the slices. Max hopped a little closer, tilting his head. "What is this squishy treasure?" he wondered. He gave it a peck. The taste burst in his mouth. Bold, tangy, salty. His feathers puffed with delight. "This is a treasure worth sharing", he squawked. With wings beating hard, Max lifted the sandwich. It dangled and wobbled, nearly slipping from his beak, but he carried it back to the nest. The nest itself was a cozy bowl of twigs and grass. His siblings crowded close, their eyes wide. "What is it?" chirped Mick, his little brother, fluffing his chest. "It smells funny," giggled Millie, his little sister, her white feathers gleaming in the sun. Max placed the sandwich proudly in the middle of the nest. "It's called a Vegemite sandwich. You should try it". They each took a tiny peck. Mick smacked his lip. "It's salty but tasty". Millie gave a happy chirp. "It's sticky but yummy". Even mother and father magpie with their glossy wings nodded in approval. The sandwich was a hit. But Father Magpie, wise and steady, tilted his head, his golden eyes narrowed. "This sandwich won't stay fresh for long," he said. "By tomorrow, it will be spoiled." Max's heart sank like a pebble. He wanted to keep this treasure forever. Mother Magpie spread her wings gently. "Some treasures aren't meant to last", she said. "They're meant to be shared". Max blinked. He imagined the sandwich turning hard and sour in the nest. This would be a waste. Sharing it meant everybody could taste it. So, Max's family lifted the sandwich together, tugging and balancing it between their beaks. They carried it to a wide flat branch where their cousins often gathered. "Come quickly, everybody," Max called, his voice ringing like a bell. "Try this treasure before it's spoiled." The air filled with the beating of wings. Black and white feathers shimmerred as cousins, aunts, and uncles, and neighbors arrived. The gum tree branch bounced with the weight of so many magpies. Together, they tore the sandwich into little pieces. The soft bread fell apart into tiny chunks, each with a sticky piece of dark Vegemite. Every magpie got a piece, no matter how small. Mick flapped his wings happily. "We're all eating the same treasure". Millie chirped with laughter. "Even the crumbs are tasty". All around the flock squawkked and chuckled, their beaks glistening as they enjoyed the feast together. That evening, as the sky melted into orange, pink, and purple, the magpies perched together in the tree. The last crumbs had vanished, but the memory glowed in Max's heart. "A treasure isn't always about keeping it," he said softly, watching the first stars blink awake. "Sometimes the best treasures are the ones you share before they're gone." His family nodded, feathers rustling gently in the twilight breeze. And from then on, Max was known not only as a finder of treasures, but as the magpie who knew the joy of sharing. And that is the story of Max the Magpie and the Vegemite Sandwich. Max learns that sometimes the best treasures aren't the ones we keep for ourselves, but the ones we share before they're gone.
