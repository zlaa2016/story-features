Once there was a vast, beautiful land that was a home to all the animals. From the largest elephant to the smallest ant, there was room for everyone. Birds sang in the trees as monkeys swung through their branches. Snakes slithered around their trunks, and giant cats crept silently below. On one small branch of one tall tree, a tiny hummingbird built her nest. She wo bits of plants and flowers together with the silk of a spider's web, and lined it with downy moss. Soon her eggs would come, and she needed a home that was both soft and strong. But just as the hummingbird finished her nest, a small spark flew on a breeze and landed on a leaf. The spark started a fire which grew and grew and grew. The animals fled to the river and stood by the safety of its bank, too scared to move, all except the hummingbird. She thought of her home in the tall tree, of the soft, strong nest beneath her, and of her eggs that would soon be coming. And she knew that she had to do something. And so the hummingbird flew to the river and filled her tiny beak with just the littlest drop of water, for that was all it would hold. Then she flew to the fire and let the drop fall. She flew straight back to the river and again took just one drop. Back and forth she flew, letting fall drop after drop after drop. All the animals watched in bewilderment. Some laughed, others shook their heads. Finally, the great wise elephant stepped forward and asked in her great wise voice, "Hummingbird, what are you doing? You cannot put out that terrible fire. You are just a small bird." The tiny hummingbird fluttered her wings and answered in her tiny voice. I am doing what I can. When this great wise elephant heard this, she looked down at her grandchildren and she thought of what the fire would take from them. The elephant bowed her head to the hummingbird. Then I will do what I can. She reached her trunk deep into the river and filled it. With the hummingbird flying above her, the elephant thundered toward the fire and sprayed it with great jets of water. When the other animals saw the tiny hummingbird and the giant elephant working together, each doing what she could, they stopped laughing. They stopped shaking their heads. Even though they were scared, they thought of their own homes, and they said to each other, "We will do what we can." The small ants did what they could. The chimpanzees and gorillas did what they could. The leopards and lions and wartthogs and arvarks and giraffes and hippos did what they could and soon every animals was working together to put out the fire. Still the fire raged and it roared. Back and forth the hummingbird flew and her wings grew very tired. Back and forth the elephant lumbered and her legs grew very tired. Back and forth all day and all night the other animals flew, ran, marched, swung, slithered, and crawled and they grew very tired. Then, as the sun rose above the treetops and the exhausted hummingbird thought she could fly no further, she let one last drop fall and the fire was gone. The hummingbird finally came to rest on the elephant's broad back and looked out at the land that was the animals home. Where on one small branch of one tall tree, her little nest was waiting for her.
