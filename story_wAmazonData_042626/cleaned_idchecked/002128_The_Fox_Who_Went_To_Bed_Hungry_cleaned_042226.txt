Deep in the English forest, when the sky turned pink and the wind grew sleepy, most animals tucked themselves into their dens. But Felix the fox had a problem. His stomach was growling louder than a thunderstorm. He had chased rabbits, sniffed for berries, and even tried fishing. Nothing. Not a crumb, not a nibble. Still, he told himself proudly. A clever fox doesn't need supper. I'll sleep just fine without it. He trotted into his den, curled up his tail, and closed his eyes. 5 minutes later, Felix's eyes popped open. Grent went his belly. He rolled onto his side. Grumble, he rolled onto his back. Rumble. He rolled again and again until his straw bed looked like a tornado had danced on it. Finally, he groaned. "Maybe, maybe a tiny snack would help." But the forest was silent. The moon glowed high. Everyone else was asleep. Felix's nose twitched. He caught the faintest whiff of something delicious. Baked bread. He followed the smell through the dark woods until he reached the old farmhouse by the river. Through the kitchen window, he saw a fresh loaf of bread cooling on the sill. "Oh," Felix sighed. "One bite and I could sleep like a baby badger." He climbed onto the windowsill, but just as he stretched his paw, "Clang!" went a pot that he bumped. The farmer's dog barked so loudly the star shook. Felix yelped and darted back into the woods. "By now, he was hungrier than ever. I can't give up, he told himself. There must be something to eat out here. He sniffed under logs, peeked into burrows, even checked a hollow tree. Nothing but leaves and moonlight. Finally, he flopped down near the pond. Maybe if I just rest my eyes, but his stomach growled again, louder than a bullfrog chorus. A soft voice came from nearby. Can't sleep, Mr. Fox. Felix looked up to see Olive the owl blinking down from a low branch. "Not a wink," he admitted. "My stomach keeps shouting louder than my thoughts," Olive chuckled. "Well, what did you expect? No one sleeps well on an empty belly." Felix huffed. "I thought being tough meant ignoring hunger." "Hm PH," said Olive. Being tough means knowing your limits and feeding yourself before it's too late. Olive fluttered down beside him and pointed toward a nearby bush. See that bramble patch? Some blackberries ripened late this year. Felix's ears perked up. Blackberries? They're tart, but they'll do. Olive said kindly. Go on, help yourself. Felix ran over, picked a few with his paw, and gobbled them gratefully. They were sour, but to him they tasted better than gold. When his belly finally stopped growling, Felix felt warmth spreading from his paws to his tail. "Thank you, Olive," he said between yawns. "I think I can sleep now," Olive smiled. "Then you've learned tonight's lesson." "Lesson?" Felix mumbled half asleep. "Never go to bed hungry," she said softly. You'll only toss and turn and maybe wake the whole forest. Felix chuckled. Next time I'll eat before bedtime. Felix padded back to his den, the moonlight lighting his way. He curled up again, but this time his stomach was full and quiet, the wind whispered through the trees, and he drifted off peacefully, dreaming of bread and berries. From that night on, Felix always made sure to find a small meal before sunset, even if it was just a handful of berries or a crunchy beetle. And whenever a younger fox bragged, "I can sleep without supper," Felix would grin and say, "Try at it once and you'll be awake till sunrise." One evening, as the stars blinked awake, Olive hooted softly from her branch. Below, Felix snorred gently, a few berry stains still on his whiskers. The forest sighed in sleepy harmony, full bellies, happy hearts, because every creature knew now a good night's sleep begins with a good meal.
