When I was six years old, I became ill with&nbsp;polio, which left me with a disability in my leg. The illness made my right leg shorter&nbsp;and thinner than the left. From then on, I always walked with a limp. This is&nbsp;part of the reason I always wore long dresses. During this time, my father was&nbsp;a beacon of hope. He was a photographer and a painter. He taught me about&nbsp;literature, nature, and philosophy. Despite the fact that sports was seen as&nbsp;unsuitable for girls, he encouraged me to play sports, to regain my strength. He also&nbsp;taught me photography. I learned how to retouch, develop, and color photographs. I started school&nbsp;later than my peers because I've been sick for so long. When I did start school, I got into&nbsp;trouble because I had a rebellious nature. But then I changed schools. It was a&nbsp; preparatory school that had only just started accepting girls. It was a big&nbsp;school, but there were only 35 children who were girls. Things started to turn around&nbsp;for me. I began enjoying learning, especially about the culture of my home nation, Mexico. I also like matters of politics and justice. I often admired the work of a&nbsp; fellow student, Diego Rivera,&nbsp;&nbsp; who was creating a mural called The Creation&nbsp; on the school campus. I'm going to marry him someday. I wanted to become a doctor, so I&nbsp; knew that I had to get good grades. Sadly, my dreams of becoming a doctor ended after a&nbsp; bad accident. I was on a bus when it crashed. The bus collided with a car, and I was injured&nbsp;very badly. A steel handrail stabbed me through the hip, and my spine and pelvis were fractured. The pain that accident caused me, followed me for the rest of my life. I was confined to a&nbsp;bed for a very long time before I could walk again. My parents encouraged me to paint and made&nbsp; a special easel for me so I could do so in bed. They, also, gave me brushes and boxes of paints&nbsp;and even put a mirror on the ceiling so I could see myself. Painting helped me kill the&nbsp;time and ease the pain I experienced. I mostly painted pictures of myself because&nbsp;I was usually alone. Painting became the best way for me to express myself, and I&nbsp;continued to paint for the rest of my life. My art began to receive recognition. My&nbsp;first solo exhibition was at the Julian Levy Gallery in New York in 1938. It was&nbsp;a success! Soon after the Louvre Museum purchased one of my paintings, The Frame, making me the first Mexican artist to be featured in their collection. I was asked&nbsp;to paint murals in many famous places. I traveled often for my art and to&nbsp; continue my efforts to campaign for social and political justice. My paintings have broken records for Latin American art. I became the&nbsp;first Latin American artist to break the $1 million threshold when, Diego and I, was&nbsp;auctioned off for over $1 million. In 2006, Roots sold for $5.6 million, and in 2016,&nbsp;Two Lovers in a Forest, sold for $8 million. Even with all the success, one of my favorite&nbsp;things to do was to teach younger students at an art school. This made me very happy. Passion&nbsp;is the bridge that takes you from pain to change.
