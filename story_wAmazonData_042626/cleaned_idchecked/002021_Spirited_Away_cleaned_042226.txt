This is Spirited Away, the highest grossing anime film of all time. It's about a sullen 10-year-old girl who moves with her parents to the suburbs. One day, while exploring their new neighborhood, she wanders into a world ruled by gods, witches, and spirits, and where humans are changed into beasts. It's up to her to find a way to save her parents and everyone else who's been taken. But how? Well, she'll have to get a job from the witch Huba, who runs a bath house that caters to these spirits. The only problem is she doesn't want to bathe dirty spirits for a living. Oh, and also, she can't die, or else she'll be trapped there forever. And if that wasn't enough, she also has to avoid this creepy demon thing that stalks her everywhere she goes. So, yeah, this movie is like an hour and a half of non-stop plot. The story is so crazy that it feels like a fever dream. Like one minute our protagonist is walking through the woods and the next she is naked and covered in mud. And then later she's working in a bath house full of weird customers. And then later she's trying to avoid a giant demon baby while riding on the back of a giant flying fish. Now, even though it's fast-paced and action-packed, the movie isn't without its slow moments, there are some beautifully animated sequences, like when Chihiro first enters the spirit world, and we get this gorgeous pan shot that takes us from the forest to the river to the sky. These slow moments help make the movie feel epic. It's not just some random things happening. There's a clear progression to the story. But what makes Spirited Away special aren't the visuals or the plot, it's the characters. From the very beginning, we see that Chihiro isn't a normal 10-year-old girl. When her family moves to the suburbs, she misses her friends and feels isolated. She doesn't want to explore their new neighborhood because she's too busy sulking and eating snacks. She doesn't really change or grow throughout the story, but at least she doesn't have to. The movie is about her learning to appreciate the people around her, not about becoming a better person. When she first starts working at the bath house, she's rude and lazy. She does not want to do any work, and she gets upset when the spirits do not tip her. But over time, we see her slowly open up and become more friendly. We get some great character development for a movie that's only 127 minutes long. I mean, there's Haku, the mysterious spirit that helps Chihiro throughout the movie. At first, he's cold and distant, but as Chihiro gets to know him, we learn that he's actually really sweet and caring. And then there's no face, the lonely spirit who becomes obsessed with Chihiro. He's one of the most tragic characters in the movie, and his arc is both heartbreaking and thoughtprovoking. The voice acting is fantastic. Roomi Hiagi does a great job voicing Chihiro.
