The Turkey Hands written and illustrated by Susan K. Fairchild. Turkey hands are fun to scroll. Some are big and some are small. Finger feathers, little thumb heads are orange, yellow, blue, and red. Scissors free the paper flock. A cage once closed, now unlocked. On the board and pinned in place. Is there mischief on that face? The teacher leaves for the night, shuts the door, turns off the light. She doesn't know the secret rule. Things come to life in an empty school. When the moon lights up the sky, turkey hands flutter and fly. Off the wall and on the desks. The turkey hands make such a mess. They dip their feathers in the ink and finger paint the once white sink. Markers, crayons, tape galore help them make new room decor. And as the turkeys play all night, they build a mighty appetite. They sip the glue and eat the paste because they truly love the taste. Gobbley goo. Gobbley goo. It's so delicious and sticky, too. Wait, what? Paste and glue. Ew. We're too smart to do that, right? reading friends. They pick and peck the alphabet that hangs above the cabinet. But paper is a little bland, even for a turkey hand. Then one spies a forgotten lunch. Tasty food for them to munch. Working together as a flock, they unpack the lone lunchbox. Gobbledegobbley goo. So much goodness to slurp and chew. Spoiled milk and sour grapes quickly give them belly aches. But the worst surprise is the last. The unwrapped sandwich makes them gasp. A turkey sandwich. What a shock. They freeze in place, eyeballs locked. Who would ever eat turkey meat? Especially when it's on whole wheat. In no time flat, they all race back to the board in a crowded pack. They scramble over books and shelves, keeping fingers to themselves. But when they see the mess they made, they realize they have misbehaved. The teacher cannot see this sight. They have till dawn to make things right. In a flash, they make a plan to clean the room and span. They hold their beaks in disgust. No turkey hand likes to dust. They pick the trash up off the floor even though their backs are sore. They wipe the scribbles off the sink and wash the lunchbox of its stink. Through the glass they spy the sun. Just in time, their chores are done. Up to the board they leap, hoping they can fall asleep. When the teacher flips the switch, the turkey hands don't move or twitch. She stares at Prince on the ground, not believing what she's found. She does not know the secret rule. Things come to life in an empty school. Even though the turkeys made a mess, I'm glad they knew they should clean up after themselves. I hope you enjoyed our story today. See you next time.
