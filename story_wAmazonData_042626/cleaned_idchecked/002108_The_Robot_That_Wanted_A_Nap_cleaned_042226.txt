In the snowy town of Costroma, where winter lasted half the year and the Vulga River froze into a glittering white road, 14-year-old Ardum Soalov was getting ready for the winter robotics tournament. Ardom loved building machines. His room was always filled with wires, spare batteries, tool kits, and little robots that beeped randomly in the middle of the night. This year he was paired with his friend Misha who was smart but well lazy. Misha preferred video games, hot chocolate, and staying under his warm blanket instead of doing actual work. But Ardum believed in him. We can win this. Ardom insisted. Misha shrugged. Yeah, maybe. We'll see. The project that wouldn't start. They were supposed to build a snow clearing robot. The parts were ready. Instructions were ready. The ideas were ready. Only Misha wasn't. Every day after school, Ardum would knock on Miesa's door. "Let's work on the robot," he'd say. Misha would yawn. "Later, I'm busy." He wasn't. He was watching videos. Artum explained the design. He drew diagrams. He even set up the entire workspace in Misha's garage. But whenever Ardom handed Misha a tool, Misha would just fiddle with it, then put it down again. Finally, frustrated, Ardam said, "Miesa, I'm trying to help you. Why won't you do anything?" Misha shrugged again. "I'll feel like working tomorrow." Ardam groaned. "You sound like my grandmother's old saying. You can lead a horse to water, but you can't make him drink." Misha laughed. Well, then I'm the horse. Deadline disaster. 2 days before the tournament, their robot still didn't work. It didn't even move. Ardom panicked. Misha, we're running out of time. Misha frowned, suddenly serious. Maybe, maybe I should have helped more. Ardam raised an eyebrow. You think? Misha sighed. For the first time, he looked guilty. Okay, teach me everything. I'm ready now. The choice that changed everything. Ardam showed him the wiring and coding. Misha listened, really listened, and asked questions. They worked late into the night, blowing warm air onto their frozen fingers. By morning, the robot's wheels spun. By afternoon, its arm lifted snow. By evening, it actually worked. Misha looked amazed. I can't believe it. I did that. Ardom smiled. I told you you're capable, but you had to want it. Tournament day. Snowflakes drifted lazily from the gray sky as dozens of kids gathered in the school gym. Each team proudly showing off their creations. When it was their turn, Ardum held his breath. Misha pressed the start button. Their robot moved smoothly across the test track, scooping snow with perfect precision. The judges nodded. Other students cheered. They didn't win first place, but they earned second. The best result their school had seen in years. Misha grinned from ear to ear. I should have tried earlier. Ardom nudged him. Well, now you know. I could push you, guide you, remind you, but only you can decide to do the work. Misha nodded deeply. I get it now. You can lead a horse to water, but you can't make him drink. Unless, he added with a smirk, the horse finally gets thirsty. Ardam laughed. Exactly.
