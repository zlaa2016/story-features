There once was a quiet country mouse. He had a cousin who lived in a big town. The town mouse came to see the country mouse. The country mouse fixed a big dinner. The two mice ate oats, corn, and nuts. But the town mouse didn't like the dinner. This food is dull, he said. I am used to fancy food. I eat the crumbs from rich dinners. I have hot rolls and cake. The country mouse licked his lips. Oh boy, he said. Hot rolls and cake. The town mouse smiled. He put his arm around his cousin. Dear cousin, come with me. He said, "I will show you how to really live." The two mice got to town that night. The town mouse took his cousin home. It was a big house with many windows. This is where I live," said the town mouse. "Now you will see how I feast." He led his cousin to a big dining room. Lots of hot food was on the table. "Wow," said the country mouse. "Look at all of the food." "Well, dear cousin," the town mouse said. "Let's eat." So they did. They tasted the sweet rolls and the cheese. The country mouse was very happy. "Oh, cousin," he said. "This is great. It's much better than the country." But just then, something awful happened. A meaneyed hairy cat jumped onto the table. "Hello, boys," he said, "Having some dinner." And he grinned a mean cat grin. The two mice were scared. Stiff. Gulp, said the town mouse. Let's get out of here. Then he jumped off the table. The country mouse was right behind him and the cat was right behind him. The two mice ran up a curtain. They slipped out of a window. Now they were safe. They stopped to catch their breath. "Who was that?" the country mouse asked. The town mouse gave a big sigh. "Oh, that's the house cat. He always chases me away from the table, but I'm used to it." "Well, I'm not." The country mouse said, "It's no fun getting chased around all the time." "This may be a nice place, but I don't like to eat and run." The country mouse stared at his cousin. I think I'm better off in the country, he said. Goodbye, cousin town mouse. The two mice shook paws. At last, the country mouse made it home. Now, his simple food tasted great. Well, it may not be hot rolls and cake, he said, but at least I can eat in peace. So, the town mouse wasn't used to where the country mouse lived, and the country mouse wasn't used to where the town mouse lived. It's all in where we grow up.
