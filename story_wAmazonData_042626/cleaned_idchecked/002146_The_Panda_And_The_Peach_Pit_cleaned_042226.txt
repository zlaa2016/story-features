The panda and the peach pit. Ping. The panda sat by the river. His round shoulders slumped. Where his grandfather's grove of sweet plum and apricot trees once stood. There was only an empty meadow, silent and bare. A heavy sigh escaped him. "All the sweetness is gone," he mumbled to a passing butterfly. and it's too late to bring it back. A slow, deliberate voice spoke from behind him. Too late is a shadow that grows only when you stand still. Ping turned to see Master Tao, the ancient tortoise, whose shell was as wrinkled as a map of the mountains. He had seen more seasons than all the trees in the forest combined. Master Tao extended a leathery foot, and in it rested a single perfect peach pit. "The best time to plant a tree was 20 years ago," the tortoise said, his voice as calm as the deep forest pools. "The second best time is now." He placed the seed in Ping's paw. With a new spark in his eyes, Ping took the seed to the center of the empty meadow. He dug a small hole with his paws, placed the seed inside, and covered it gently with earth. It was just one tiny seed in a very large, very empty field. His friends, Ling, the monkey, and May the red panda, watched from a nearby bamboo stock. You're planting a rock, Ping, chattered Ling, swinging by his tail. You'll be old and gray like Master Tao before that thing even sprouts a leaf. May just shook her head, thinking it was a waste of a perfectly good nap time. Ping ignored them. Every day he brought water from the river. He pulled the weeds that tried to crowd his little spot. He protected the ground from the hot sun and the cold nights. He waited. He was patient. And slowly, slowly, the seed responded. A sprout became a sapling. and the sapling stretched its branches towards the sky. Seasons passed. The sapling grew into a strong, healthy tree. One spring, it burst into a cloud of fragrant pink blossoms. By the end of summer, the branches were heavy with fuzzy golden red peaches. The scent was intoxicating. Ping picked the first ripe fruit and took a bite. It was the sweetest thing he had ever tasted. He gathered baskets full and called out to all the animals in the forest. Ling and Mi came, their eyes wide with disbelief. Everyone came. Ping shared the fruit freely, and the meadow was filled with the happy sounds of munching and laughter. "You were right, Ping," Ling said, his mouth full of peach. "This is much better than a rock." The next day, Ping didn't just tend to his tree. He gathered the pits from the eaten fruit. When the younger animals, like a little rabbit named Bao, came to ask for a peach, Ping gave them one. But he also gave them a seed and showed them how to plant it. The empty meadow was no longer empty. It was becoming a forest of hope, one seed at a time.
