The boy who wouldn't give up. Eli watched them from the park bench. A blur of spinning wheels and flying colors under the cherry blossoms. The skateboarders of Sakura Park were like birds, soaring and dipping with an ease that seemed like magic. More than anything, he wanted to fly with them. For his birthday, his grandmother gave him a skateboard. It was beautiful with a deck the color of the evening sky and wheels like bright orange suns. It felt awkward and wobbly under his feet, nothing like the graceful boards he saw in the park. His first week was a symphony of scrapes and stumbles. His knees were bruised, his elbows were patched with bandages, and his beautiful board was already scuffed. Maybe I'm just not meant to do this," he sighed, plopping down on the curb. Just then, an older boy glided to a stop beside him. "Tough first day," he asked, smiling. He moved with the same easy grace Eli had admired. "My name is Ren. Everyone falls. The secret isn't not falling. It's getting up one more time than you fall." Ren pointed to Eli's feet. "You're standing too stiff. Bend your knees. Be like a willow tree, not an old oak. Feel the board become a part of you. He demonstrated, crouching low and weaving effortlessly down the path. Eli listened. He took a deep breath, bent his knees, and pushed off. He wobbled. He swayed, but for three glorious seconds, he rolled. A huge grin spread across his face before he tipped over, laughing, onto a soft patch of grass. It wasn't flying, but it was a start. Every day after school, Eli was at the park. He practiced pushing and turning, his balance getting better with each try. He still fell, but now the falls were followed by a quicker bounce back to his feet. Ren would often be there, offering a quiet nod of encouragement. Then came the Ali, the trick that made the board jump into the air. It looked like true magic. For weeks, Eli tried to master it. He'd stomp his back foot, slide his front foot, and jump. But the board would only clatter uselessly on the ground. Frustration began to creep back in. "You're thinking too much," Ren said one afternoon, seeing his struggle. "Just feel the timing. Pop, slide, jump, all one motion." He showed Eli again, his board leaping into the air as if tied to his feet by an invisible string. Eli closed his eyes and pictured it. Pop, slide, jump. He took a breath, opened his eyes, and tried again. He stomped his back foot, felt the tail of the board hit the pavement, slid his front foot up, and jumped with all his might. For a breathtaking moment, the world went silent. He was airborne. The board right there with him. He landed with a loud, satisfying clack of wheels on concrete. He stumbled, but didn't fall. He had done it. He had flown. From across the park, he heard a cheer. It was Ren, giving him a thumbs up. Eli grinned. the biggest grin of his life and pushed off, rolling smoothly towards his friend. He wasn't a master yet, but he was a skateboarder, and he knew he would never give up.
