Duchi twirled to the sounds that poured into the plaza letting the music kiss her skin. She closed her eyes, lifted her imaginary guitar, and swayed. "I wish I could be a mariachi," she said as her knees bounced to the rousing rhythms. "It' be fantastico with its sweet cinnamon melodies like silky swirls of honey poured over a stack of mamasittita's fried bignellos." Her music would wave over the plaza, too, and move people to sing, dance, and clap. In bed that night, her eyes drink in the moonlight. Tuchi whispered, "Hear my soulful wish, Seenora Luna, make me a mariachi." At school the next day, Mr. Sanchez announced, "I'm starting a mariachi ensemble. Auditions are in two months." He then tucked his violin under his chin and played. After the final quivering note, everyone cheered. Tuchi sighed. Mr. Sanchez, you're a real mariachi. Thank you. He handed out flyers. Tui stuck her hand out to take a flyer, but he chuckled. I did not mean you, Tui. Mariachi is only for boys. Her hand dropped back to her side. Saturday evening, Tata's friends gathered and brought their instruments. One man held a vue. The shimmering pluck strings drew Tuchi closer. "Mr. Sanchez must have made a mistake," she thought. She drew up her courage and asked, "Can I try?" "Sorry, Na. You need strength and stamina. It's just for Omres." Duchi flexed her muscles and said, "I am strong." But the men dismissed her with laughs. Disappointed, Tuchi slipped away and hid in the storage room. The men's music and grios filled the air like sparks from burning logs. An ember stirred inside her, longing to be free. How could Mr. Sanchez be right if she felt the music so deeply. Did the music belong to everyone? Suddenly, a sliver of moon beam glimmered on a large case clapped shut. What's this? Tuchi shook it and a deep twang vibrated. She pried the case open and gasped. A guitar. Tuchi could still hear the sounds of Tata and his mariachi friends as they played song after song. songs about being in love and poor, being lonely and sad, and being happy and free. All these feelings whirled into her song, a song about being duchi. She pulled the guitaron out of its case. She strummed to the mariachi's tunes and rocked to the driving rhythms as they shifted in sache. The sound of melodic ribbons embroidered the night sky. The next morning, Tucci dashed to the kitchen to find your nana. Did you know there's a garon in the storage room? Tui asked. I forgot it was there. That was made for me when I was your age. I learned to play, but they never let me join the mariachi. Women were not allowed. They told me I could sing, but I wanted to play. Sounds familiar. Tui frowned. Could you teach me? It's time things change. Let's see what I remember. Nana tuned the strings and played a few chords. The deepest sound ever like the low growl of desert thunder. My turn. Nana bueno. Remember, always play to corasonto with all my heart. Tui squeezed her eyes shut and listened to all the rancheras, bolto, and coridos cocooned inside her. Then she strummed. In her dreams, Tucci swayed with the guitaron and ended her song with a sweet shimmering court like a sigh from a falling star. Tui looked up at the sky. Seenor Luna port favor. I really want to be a mariachi more than ever. Whenever she could, Tucci practiced a guitar with her nana. She remembered the deadline Mr. Sanchez given for auditions. But will she be ready in time? Trust your ears. Horando, you're improving. We win to chi. Soon she could play the bass part on a few songs. Trumpa trumpa. And like a good mariachi, she let loose her wild gito. The day arrived. They packed Tata's truck and drove her to the school. Head high, Tui marched into the classroom. The boys spotted her and told her, "Leave. Go home, Ti. You don't belong here." No way. Boys only. The word is mariachi, not mariacho. Tuchi said, "What's going on? I know I'm a girl." Tuchi planted her feet, but I deserve a chance to be on the ensemble. Some of the boys snickered. Toi's shoulders sank, but she remembered Nana's word about how things needed to change. And so, she stood strong like a saro. Mr. Sanchez sat back. I'm sorry, but this group is all boys. I can play. Let me prove it. Without waiting for a reply, Duchi began to play, but her fingers fumbled. She forgotten how to pluck a pizzicado that echoed like mountain heartbeats. No drun sound did. Ignoring the stairs, Tuti remember Senor Luna. She took a deep breath and strummed her drum papas. Then the ember in her heart grew into a fire. She burst into a rancher contouas. The boys grew silent. Mr. Sanchez leaped up and grabbed his violin again. Tuchi, play it again. She did and his violin soared over her bass part. Tuchi let out a gri. This time the boys applauded and a few even whistled and shouted and tui took to. That night, Tuchi opened her window and breathed in the moonlight. Muchas gracias, Seenora Luna. And then she began to strum her guitar on straight from her chorus, for she was now Duchi La Mariachi.
