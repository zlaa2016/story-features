Once upon a time there was a young&nbsp; boy, and his name was Little Sambha. And his mother was called Maata. And his father was called Baapa. One day Maata made him a&nbsp; beautiful little red coat,&nbsp;&nbsp; and a pair of beautiful little blue trousers. And Baapa went to the bazaar,&nbsp; and bought him a beautiful green&nbsp;&nbsp; umbrella, and a lovely little pair of purple&nbsp; shoes with crimson soles and crimson linings. And then wasn't Little Sambha grand? So he put on all his fine clothes,&nbsp; and went out for a walk in the jungle.&nbsp;&nbsp; By and by, he met a tiger and the tiger said to&nbsp; him, "Little Sambha, I'm going to eat you up!" And Little Sambha said, "Oh!&nbsp; Please, Mr. Tiger, don't eat me up,&nbsp;&nbsp; and I'll give you my beautiful little red coat." So the tiger said, "Very well,&nbsp; I won't eat you this time,&nbsp;&nbsp; but you must give me your&nbsp; beautiful little red coat." So the tiger got poor Little Sambha's beautiful&nbsp; little red coat, and went away saying,&nbsp;&nbsp; "Now I'm the grandest tiger in the jungle." And Little Sambha walked on and by and by&nbsp; he met another tiger, and he said to him,&nbsp;&nbsp; "Little Sambha, I'm going to eat you up!" And Little Sambha said, "Oh! Please, Mr. Tiger,&nbsp; don't eat me up, and I'll give you my beautiful&nbsp;&nbsp; little blue trousers." So the tiger got poor&nbsp; Little Sambha's beautiful little blue trousers,&nbsp;&nbsp; and went away saying, "Now I'm&nbsp; the grandest tiger in the jungle." And Little Sambha went on and by and by&nbsp; he met another tiger, and it said to him,&nbsp;&nbsp; "Little Sambha, I'm going to eat you up!" And Little Sambha said, "Oh! Please,&nbsp; Mr. Tiger, don't eat me up, and I'll&nbsp;&nbsp; give you my beautiful little purple shoes&nbsp; with crimson soles and crimson linings." But the tiger said, "What use&nbsp; would your shoes be to me?&nbsp;&nbsp; I have four feet and you have only two.&nbsp; You haven't got enough shoes for me." But Little Sambha said, "You&nbsp; could wear them on your ears." "So I could," said the tiger,&nbsp;&nbsp; "that's a very good idea. Give them&nbsp; to me and I won't eat you this time." So the tiger got poor Little Sambha's&nbsp; beautiful little purple shoes with crimson&nbsp;&nbsp; soles and crimson linings and went away saying,&nbsp;&nbsp; "Now I'm the grandest tiger in the jungle." And by and by Little Sambha met another tiger,&nbsp;&nbsp; and he said to him, "Little&nbsp; Sambha, I'm going to eat you up!" And Little Sambha said, "Oh!&nbsp; Please, Mr. Tiger,&nbsp;&nbsp; don't eat me up, and I'll give&nbsp; you my beautiful green umbrella." But the tiger said, "How can I carry an&nbsp; umbrella when I need all my paws for walking?" "You could tie a knot on your tail, and&nbsp; carry it that way," said Little Sambha. "So I could," said the tiger. "Give it&nbsp; to me, and I won't eat you this time." So the tiger got poor Little&nbsp; Sambha's beautiful green&nbsp;&nbsp; umbrella and went away saying, "Now&nbsp; I'm the grandest tiger in the jungle." And Little Sambha went away crying, because the&nbsp; cruel tigers had taken all his fine clothes. Presently, he heard a horrible noise&nbsp; that sounded like "Gr-r-r-r-rrr," and it got louder and louder. "Oh! Dear!" said Little Sambha. "That must&nbsp; be all the tigers coming back to eat me up!&nbsp;&nbsp; What shall I do?" He ran quickly to a palm tree&nbsp; and peeped around it to see what the matter was. There he saw all the tigers arguing and&nbsp; disputing which of them was the grandest.&nbsp;&nbsp; At last they all got so angry that they knew the&nbsp; only way to settle the argument was to fight.&nbsp;&nbsp; They took off all the fine&nbsp; clothes, so as not to ruin them,&nbsp;&nbsp; and began to tear each other with their claws and&nbsp; bite each other with their great big white teeth. And they came, rolling and tumbling right&nbsp; to the foot of the very tree where Little&nbsp;&nbsp; Sambha was hiding, but he jumped&nbsp; quickly to hide behind the umbrella. The tigers all caught hold of each other's&nbsp; tails as they wrangled and scrambled,&nbsp;&nbsp; and so they found themselves&nbsp; in a ring round the tree. Then, while the tigers were wrangling and&nbsp; scrambling, Little Sambha jumped up, and called&nbsp;&nbsp; out, "Oh, tigers! Why have you taken off all&nbsp; your nice clothes? Don't you want them anymore?" But the tigers only answered, "Gr-r-r!" Then Little Sambha said, "If you want them, say&nbsp; so, or else I'll take them away." But the tigers&nbsp;&nbsp; would not let go of each other's tails and&nbsp; so they could only say, "Grrr-r-r-r-r-r-rrrr!" So Little Sambha put all his fine&nbsp; clothes on again and walked off. The tigers were very, very angry but still&nbsp; they would not let go of each other's tails.&nbsp;&nbsp; They were so angry that they ran around the tree&nbsp; trying to eat each other up, and they ran faster&nbsp;&nbsp; and faster until they were whirling around&nbsp; so fast you couldn't see their legs at all. And they still ran faster and faster and&nbsp; faster until they all just melted away&nbsp;&nbsp; and there was nothing left but a&nbsp; great big pool of melted butter&nbsp;&nbsp; (or ghi, as it is called in India)&nbsp; around the base of the tree. Now Baapa was just coming home from&nbsp; his work with a great big brass pot&nbsp;&nbsp; in his arms and when he saw what&nbsp; was left of all the tigers he said,&nbsp;&nbsp; "Oh! What lovely melted butter! I'll take&nbsp; that home to Maata for her to use in cooking." So he put it all into the great big brass&nbsp; pot, and took it home to Maata to cook with. When Maata saw the melted&nbsp; butter, wasn't she pleased! "Now we'll all have pancakes&nbsp; for supper!" she said. So she got flour and eggs and milk and sugar&nbsp; and butter, and she made a huge big plate of&nbsp;&nbsp; the loveliest pancakes. She fried them in&nbsp; the melted butter that the tigers had made,&nbsp;&nbsp; and they were just as yellow&nbsp; and brown as little tigers. And then they all sat down to supper. And Maata ate twenty-seven pancakes. And Baapa ate fifty-five. And Little Sambha ate A HUNDRED AND&nbsp; SIXTY-NINE, because he was so hungry.
